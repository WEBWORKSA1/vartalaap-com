The 30 questions

What interviewers really ask — and how to structure the answer

HR & personality

1. Tell me about yourself.

Present (who you are now) → past (one proof point with a number) → future (why this role). 45–60 seconds. Use the builder above.

2. What are your strengths?

Pick two that the job needs. For each, give one short story: situation, what you did, measurable result.

3. What is your biggest weakness?

A real but non-fatal weakness + the system you use to manage it + evidence it's improving. Avoid “I'm a perfectionist”.

4. Why do you want to work here?

One fact about the company (product, customer, mission) + how your skill fits it + what you want to learn there.

5. Where do you see yourself in five years?

Show ambition inside their world: deeper expertise, bigger responsibility, leading a small team or product area.

6. Why should we hire you?

Summarise: you can do the work (proof), you'll fit the team (behaviour), and you're motivated by this role (reason).

7. Why are you leaving your current job?

Future-focused, never negative: growth, scope, a new domain. One sentence, then pivot to why this role.

8. What are your salary expectations?

Give a researched range, anchored slightly above your target, and say you're flexible for the right role and total package.

9. Tell me about a time you failed.

STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result — then add “what I do differently now”. The learning is the answer.

10. Describe a conflict with a colleague.

Focus on the issue, not the person. Show you listened, found common ground and protected the outcome.

11. How do you handle pressure?

Name your method (prioritise, break into steps, communicate early) and give one deadline story.

12. Are you a team player?

Don't just say yes. Tell a story where you helped someone else succeed.

13. What motivates you?

Choose something the role offers: solving customer problems, learning fast, visible impact.

14. Do you have any questions for us?

Always yes. Ask about success in the first 90 days, the team's biggest challenge, or how performance is measured.

15. Explain a gap in your resume.

Be brief and honest. Mention what you did in that time (care, study, project) and why you're ready now.

Freshers, skills & situations

16. Walk me through your final-year project.

Problem → your role → tools → one hard decision → result. Expect follow-up questions on every claim.

17. Why did you choose this field?

One genuine moment of interest + what you've done since to build skill.

18. What did you learn from your internship?

One technical skill and one workplace skill, each with an example.

19. How do you keep learning?

Name specific sources and one thing you learnt recently and applied.

20. Are you willing to relocate or work shifts?

Answer honestly. If yes, say so clearly; if conditional, state the condition politely.

21. What would you do in your first 30 days?

Learn (people, product, process), deliver one small win, then propose one improvement.

22. How would you explain [your skill] to a non-expert?

Use an everyday analogy. Clarity matters more than jargon.

23. Tell me about a time you led something.

Leadership is influence, not title: a club event, a group project, a process you fixed.

24. How do you prioritise multiple tasks?

Urgent vs important, deadlines and impact. Mention a tool (list, calendar, tracker).

25. What do you know about our competitors?

Name two, one difference each, and what that means for this company.

26. How do you handle criticism?

Listen, ask for specifics, act, follow up. Give one example of feedback that made you better.

27. Describe your ideal manager.

Clear expectations, regular feedback, room to own work — traits most managers want to be seen with.

28. Sell me this pen.

Ask questions first: what do they use, what frustrates them. Then sell the benefit, not the features.

29. What makes you unique?

A combination: a skill + a background + a habit that together fit this role.

30. Anything else you'd like us to know?

One-line recap of your fit and a thank-you. End with energy.

Phrases that win a GD

  • Open: “Let me start by defining what we mean by…”
  • Add value: “To build on Rohan's point, one data point we haven't discussed is…”
  • Disagree politely: “I see it differently, and here's why…”
  • Bring in a quiet member: “Meera, we haven't heard your view yet.”
  • Summarise: “So the group seems to agree on X, while Y is still open.”
