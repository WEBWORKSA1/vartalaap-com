IELTS speaking: how to raise your band

Exams · 9 min read · Updated October 2026

The IELTS speaking test is a face-to-face conversation of about 11–14 minutes with an examiner. It rewards natural, extended speech far more than memorised answers.

The three parts

PartLengthWhat happensStrategy
1. Introduction & interview4–5 minQuestions on familiar topics: home, work, study, hobbies.Answer + reason + example. Two to three sentences.
2. Long turn3–4 minA task card; 1 minute to prepare, then speak for up to 2 minutes.Use all your time. Tell a story with detail.
3. Discussion4–5 minAbstract questions linked to Part 2.Give opinions, compare, speculate, justify.

The four criteria (25% each)

  • Fluency and coherence — speaking at length without unnatural pauses, linking ideas logically.
  • Lexical resource — range and precision of vocabulary, including less common words and collocations.
  • Grammatical range and accuracy — mixing simple and complex sentences with few errors.
  • Pronunciation — being easy to understand: sounds, word stress, sentence stress and intonation. Accent itself is not penalised.

Eight tips examiners would tell you

  1. Extend every answer. One-word or one-sentence answers cap your fluency score.
  2. Don't memorise. Examiners are trained to spot rehearsed answers and will change the topic.
  3. Paraphrase the question rather than repeating it word for word.
  4. Use natural fillers sparingly. “That's an interesting question” buys time once; “um” ten times costs marks.
  5. Show range: conditionals (“If I had more time, I'd…”), comparisons, past and future forms.
  6. Use topic vocabulary precisely — “commute”, “work-life balance”, “sustainable”.
  7. Self-correct naturally if you make a mistake — it shows control.
  8. Ask for repetition if needed: “Sorry, could you repeat the question?” is acceptable.

A three-week plan

Week 1: Record Part 1 answers on 15 common topics. Check pace and fillers with the pace meter.
Week 2: Do one Part 2 card daily with a strict 1-minute prep and 2-minute talk. Use the just-a-minute timer.
Week 3: Full mock tests with a partner or coach, focusing on Part 3 opinion questions.
