25 common Indian-English mistakes — and what to say instead

Spoken English · 8 min read · Updated October 2026

Indian English is a legitimate variety of English, used by hundreds of millions of people. But some of its phrases confuse — or quietly put off — recruiters, clients and colleagues abroad. If your audience is global, these are worth adjusting.

Paste any email or answer into our free Indian-English fixer to catch these patterns automatically.

Workplace phrases

#Instead ofSayWhy
1Please do the needful.Please approve the leave by Friday.Vague and dated. Name the action.
2Kindly revert.Please reply / get back to me.“Revert” means return to a previous state.
3Let's prepone the meeting.Let's bring the meeting forward.“Prepone” isn't used outside South Asia.
4I have a doubt.I have a question.“Doubt” suggests you don't believe something.
5Let's discuss about the budget.Let's discuss the budget.“Discuss” already contains “about”.
6I am out of station.I'm out of town / travelling.“Station” is Indian usage.
7Please find the attached file herewith.I've attached the file.Shorter and more natural.
8Myself Rahul from Accounts.This is Rahul from Accounts.“Myself” is a reflexive pronoun.
9Kindly do the same.Please send it to me as well.Say what “the same” is.
10Pls / PlzPleaseToo casual for professional messages.

Grammar patterns from Hindi

#Instead ofSayWhy
11I am having two brothers.I have two brothers.“Have” for possession isn't continuous.
12I am working here since 2021.I have been working here since 2021.Ongoing action from a past point uses present perfect continuous.
13I am living here since 3 years.I've lived here for 3 years.“For” + duration, “since” + starting point.
14Where you are going?Where are you going?Direct questions invert the verb.
15I didn't went.I didn't go.After “did/didn't”, use the base verb.
16He don't know.He doesn't know.He/she/it takes “does”.
17I am agree.I agree.“Agree” is a verb, not an adjective.
18More betterBetter / much betterDouble comparative.
19Explain me this.Explain this to me.Explain something to someone.
20Many informations / advicesSome information / adviceUncountable nouns have no plural.

Everyday phrases

#Instead ofSayWhy
21I passed out of college in 2022.I graduated in 2022.“Pass out” means to faint.
22What is your good name?May I know your name?A literal translation of “shubh naam”.
23My cousin brotherMy cousin“Cousin” covers both genders.
24Return back / revert backReturn / replyRedundant.
25Cope up withCope withThe phrasal verb is “cope with”.

How to fix them for good

Knowing the rule is easy; using it under pressure is hard. Pick three items from these tables that you personally say, write the correct version on a sticky note, and use each one out loud five times a day for a week. Then record a one-minute answer and listen for them. After two weeks, choose the next three.
