Basic Hindi conversation for beginners

Hindi · 10 min read · Updated October 2026

Whether you're joining a Hindi-speaking family, moving to India or reconnecting with your heritage, you can hold a simple conversation within a few weeks. Here's what matters most at the start.

1. Politeness is built into the language

Hindi has three words for “you”, and choosing the right one matters more than perfect grammar:

WordUse withExample
आप (aap)Elders, in-laws, strangers, anyone you respectआप कैसे हैं? — Aap kaise hain?
तुम (tum)Friends, people your age, younger familyतुम कैसे हो? — Tum kaise ho?
तू (tu)Very close friends, small children — can sound rude otherwiseतू कैसा है? — Tu kaisa hai?
Beginner rule: when in doubt, use aap. Nobody is offended by too much respect.

Adding ji after a name or title (Sharma ji, Maa ji) is another simple way to show respect.

2. Sentence structure: verb at the end

English is subject–verb–object (“I eat food”). Hindi is subject–object–verb: मैं खाना खाता हूँ — Main khaana khaata hoon (literally “I food eat am”). Once you accept that the verb waits until the end, Hindi sentences become much easier to build.

3. Verbs change with gender

In Hindi, the verb often reflects the speaker's gender:

  • A man says: मैं सीख रहा हूँ — Main seekh raha hoon (I am learning).
  • A woman says: मैं सीख रही हूँ — Main seekh rahi hoon.

The pattern is usually -a for masculine and -i for feminine. Don't let this stop you speaking; people will understand either way and appreciate the effort.

5. A four-week starter routine

  1. Week 1: greetings and the aap/tum system. Use “Namaste” and “Aap kaise hain?” with every Hindi speaker you meet.
  2. Week 2: food and family phrases. Practise the “Visiting the in-laws” scene in the Conversation Lab.
  3. Week 3: shopping, numbers and directions — run the market and auto-rickshaw scenes.
  4. Week 4: try a five-minute conversation entirely in Hindi with a friend or tutor.
