“Tell me about yourself”: the 60-second formula

Interviews · 8 min read · Updated October 2026

It's the first question in almost every interview, and it sets the tone for everything after. The interviewer isn't asking for your life story. They're asking: why should I keep listening?

The formula: present → past → future

  1. Present (10–15 seconds): who you are professionally right now.
  2. Past (20–30 seconds): one or two achievements that prove you can do this job — with numbers.
  3. Future (10–15 seconds): why this role and this company are your logical next step.

That's 45–60 seconds, or roughly 110–150 words. Shorter feels unprepared; longer loses attention.

Example: fresher

“I'm Ananya, a final-year B.Tech student in Information Technology at a college in Pune. Over the last year I've built two projects I'm proud of — an attendance app now used by about 1,200 students, and a chatbot for our placement cell that answers routine questions. Building them taught me Python, basic cloud deployment and, honestly, how to explain technical ideas to non-technical people. I'm excited about this graduate engineer role because your team builds products for first-time internet users, and I'd love to work on software that has to be simple to be useful.”

Example: experienced professional

“I'm Rohit, and for the past four years I've worked in inside sales for a B2B software company. I currently manage around 60 mid-sized accounts, and last year I grew renewals by 18% by introducing quarterly check-in calls. Before that, I spent two years in customer support, which is where I learnt to listen before I pitch. I'm now looking to move into a field sales role with larger clients, and your expansion into the education sector is exactly the kind of market I want to build in.”

What to leave out

  • Family details — “My father is a businessman and my mother is a homemaker” uses up time and adds nothing for the employer.
  • Your entire resume — they have it. Pick highlights.
  • Hobbies — unless directly relevant to the role.
  • Memorised speech tone — if it sounds recited, the interviewer stops listening.

Five mistakes that cost candidates

  1. Starting with “Myself…” Say “I'm…” or “My name is…”.
  2. No numbers. “I improved sales” is forgettable; “I improved renewals by 18%” is not.
  3. Ending weakly. Your last sentence should point to the job: why you want it.
  4. Speaking too fast. Nerves speed you up. Aim for a calm 130 words per minute — test yourself with the pace meter.
  5. Filling silence with “um”. A one-second pause sounds thoughtful; fillers sound uncertain.

How to practise it

Write it, then say it out loud five times. On the sixth time, put the paper away and speak from the three headings only: present, past, future. Record yourself and listen once. Then run the HR interview scene in the Conversation Lab to rehearse the follow-up questions.
